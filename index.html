Nicholas Wilson, Ed.D.
Learning scientist and builder. Director of Learning Innovation at Harvard University.

What counts as evidence that someone is learning, and how should a system, or a designer, respond to it?

I have worked on that question for more than 15 years across K-12 classrooms, university teaching, and workforce training: as a physics teacher, as a learning sciences researcher, as a co-principal investigator on NSF-funded augmented reality tutors, as the director of university-wide teaching innovation at Harvard, and now as Director of Learning Innovation in Harvard's Office of the Vice Provost for Advances in Learning.

Selected work

  1. 2026 to present
    Office of the Vice Provost for Advances in Learning, Harvard

    Measuring Learning in Self-Paced Courses

    Which observable behaviors justify a claim that a learner understands something, and how should a system report the absence of such evidence?

    An evidence model for contingent reasoning in courses without assignments or instructor-student interaction, which attaches an explicit inferential distance to every link between a signal and a claim and is built entirely from teaching elements the platform already supports.

  2. 2026 to present
    Office of the Vice Provost for Advances in Learning, Harvard

    AI-Assisted Course Authoring as Workplace Learning

    When an AI authoring system becomes part of how course teams learn their craft, which parts of the work should remain with them?

    Design-based research on the Course Building Assistant, a multi-agent course-design system used for Harvard Online course builds, in which the practice of instructional designers and technologists redirected the system's design three times.

  3. 2026
    Office of the Vice Provost for Advances in Learning, Harvard

    An AI-Assisted Tool for Activity-Theoretic Qualitative Analysis

    How might AI assist with qualitative analysis without displacing the researcher's judgment or compromising participants' privacy?

    A research tool that codes focus-group data through Cultural-Historical Activity Theory in successive passes, each confirmed by the researcher, with a guard against routing structural problems to training and pseudonymization that keeps participants' names away from the model.

  4. 2026
    Office of the Vice Provost for Advances in Learning, Harvard

    A Blind, Evidence-Anchored Evaluator for Course Design

    If a generative model cannot reliably evaluate its own work, what should a discriminator for course design look like?

    A standalone LLM-as-judge that reviews finished courses blind across pedagogical fidelity, learning science, and institutional quality, anchoring every finding to quoted evidence and translating each gap into a specific, platform-level recommendation.

  5. 2026
    Office of the Vice Provost for Advances in Learning, Harvard

    Faithful Course Migration Between Learning Platforms

    How do we move a course between platforms without quietly losing the design decisions that went into it?

    A deterministic migration tool that converts courses from Harvard's learning platform into edX packages and accounts for every element, so that nothing a faculty member or designer created disappears without notice.

  6. 2019 to 2023
    Northeastern University

    From Assistance to Adaptive Scaffolding: Augmented Reality Tutors for Psychomotor Skill

    How should a tutoring system respond once a learner no longer needs its support?

    NSF-funded research in which an AR guide that reduced assembly errors by 31 to 84 percent proved to function as training, which redirected the work toward modeling expertise as a basis for scaffolding that fades.

Guiding principles

The principles below guide my work, and each is grounded in a specific project.

Understanding the activity before designing for it
Before designing staff AI training, I led an inventory of how teams in my office actually used AI. The gaps staff described concerned rules, guidance, and sources of help more than the tools themselves, and the course was designed around those gaps.
Allowing learners' practice to revise the design
Participants in the first AR study outgrew the guide after two or three attempts, and course teams consistently arrived with manuscripts they had already written. Each observation led to a substantial reorganization of the system it concerned.
Deciding where human judgment matters most before deciding what to automate
Instructional technologists described building pages by hand as the point at which they evaluate each teaching choice. The design response automated verification, kept construction with people by default, and made further automation optional.
Assigning judgment to models and guarantees to code
In AI authoring systems, models decide what to teach and how to express it, while citations, structure, and platform constraints are verified deterministically and reviewers concentrate on pedagogical quality.
Treating research as a constraint where it is robust and as a hypothesis where AI changes its conditions
Worked examples, retrieval practice, and feedback shape course design directly, while assessment practices developed for learners without capable assistants are treated as hypotheses to retest.
Holding designs provisionally
I removed a capstone component from the staff AI course when it conflicted with the course's time constraints, and reorganized the Course Building Assistant around manuscript import, which displaced the generation-first workflow it began with.
Asking whether a technology narrows or widens gaps between learners
This question began with my dissertation research on technology integration and student use in a one-to-one laptop school, and it continues to shape how I evaluate learning technology.

Research directions

  • Assessing the revision of reasoning. Learners commit to a position, consult an AI, and explain whether and why their position changed, and the quality of that explanation serves as the evidence.
  • Tutors that elicit commitment before support. An AI tutor would ask for a prediction or rationale before revealing anything, track how confidence and reasoning change across situations, and reduce its support as those signals strengthen.
  • Competency graphs that represent uncertainty. Learner state would be represented per competency together with the inferential distance behind each estimate, and agent behavior would draw on that state.
  • Judgment as a competency. A staff AI competency framework developed in my office credits a well-reasoned decision not to use AI as highly as effective adoption.
  • Multivocal course design. A framework in development identifies where each discipline's perspective belongs in a course narrative, so that faculty from different fields who were never recorded together can contribute to a single coherent argument.
